If you've searched for spray foam rig pricing, you've probably noticed the numbers you find vary enormously — and not because anyone's lying to you. Rig cost genuinely spans a wide real range, and understanding what actually drives that spread matters more than finding one definitive number.

## New vs. Used Is the Biggest Single Swing Factor

A well-maintained used rig, particularly one where the proportioner has a documented service history and reasonable hour count, can cost meaningfully less than a comparable new setup — often the single biggest lever available to someone trying to enter the trade at a lower upfront cost. The risk tradeoff is real too: a used proportioner with unknown maintenance history is a genuine gamble, which is why service documentation matters more than the sticker price when evaluating a used rig.

## Output Capacity Drives Price Within Both New and Used

Within either the new or used category, output capacity (how many pounds per minute the proportioner can deliver) is the next biggest price driver. Higher-output machines cost more because they use larger pumps and more robust heating systems capable of sustaining that output — and, as covered on the proportioner buying guide, buying more capacity than your realistic job mix needs adds cost without adding value.

## Trailer vs. Truck-Mount Changes the Total, Not Just the Equipment Line

A truck-mounted configuration's total cost includes the vehicle itself (or the cost of converting/outfitting a vehicle you already own), which changes the total investment picture compared to a trailer rig that can hitch to an existing suitable tow vehicle. Comparing a "rig price" between a trailer quote and a truck-mount quote without accounting for this is a common way people end up confused about why quotes look so different.

## Supporting Equipment Is Easy to Undercount

As covered in the rig buildout checklist, generator, heated hoses, spray gun, air compressor, and drum handling setup are all real costs beyond the proportioner and trailer/truck themselves. A quote that only covers the proportioner and bare trailer, compared against a quote that includes the full buildout, will look artificially far apart — always confirm exactly what's included before comparing two numbers.

## What a Realistic Budgeting Approach Actually Looks Like

Rather than searching for a single "how much does a rig cost" answer, a more useful approach is: decide trailer vs. truck-mount based on your service radius and job type (covered in that topic guide), decide realistic output capacity based on your actual planned job mix, decide new vs. used based on your risk tolerance and access to a trusted used-equipment inspection, and then get itemized quotes that clearly state what supporting equipment is and isn't included. That process gets you a real number for your specific situation — which is more useful than any single figure a generic article could honestly give you.

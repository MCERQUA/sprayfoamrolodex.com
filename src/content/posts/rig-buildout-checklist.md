The proportioner gets most of the attention in equipment conversations, but a functional spray foam rig needs a full supporting buildout around it. Skipping or underbudgeting any one of these items creates a real operational gap on day one, not a minor inconvenience.

## The Enclosed Trailer Itself

Beyond basic size and axle-rating requirements to legally and safely haul the equipment weight, the trailer needs adequate ventilation (spray foam chemicals and equipment generate heat and require airflow), proper electrical setup for the equipment running inside it, and enough interior organization (shelving, drum mounts, hose reel mounts) to keep the space functional rather than a jumbled mess that slows down every job's setup and breakdown.

## Generator and Power

Most rigs need an onboard generator sized to run the proportioner, air compressor, lighting, and any other powered equipment simultaneously — undersizing the generator is a common rookie mistake that shows up as equipment underperformance or nuisance shutdowns mid-job, not an obvious failure you'd catch before it costs you a workday.

## Heated Hoses and Hose Reels

The heated hose system that carries both chemical components from the proportioner to the gun is a real, separate line item from the proportioner itself — hose length needs to match your typical job site layout, and hose condition directly affects ratio accuracy at the gun end, making hose maintenance and eventual replacement a genuine ongoing cost, not a one-time purchase.

## Spray Gun and Gun Maintenance Supplies

The spray gun itself, plus the cleaning solvents, replacement tips, and seal kits needed to keep it functioning correctly, are consumable-adjacent costs that a new operator's budget should include from day one — a poorly maintained gun produces inconsistent spray patterns regardless of how well-calibrated the proportioner upstream is.

## Air Compressor and Air System

Many proportioner and gun setups require a compressed air supply for gun operation and equipment purging — sized correctly for your specific equipment's air demand, which varies by brand and model and is worth confirming directly with your equipment supplier rather than assuming a generic compressor will do.

## Drum Handling and Chemical Storage

Safe, code-compliant handling and short-term storage for the chemical drums or totes — including appropriate temperature control given real shelf-life sensitivity — is part of a complete buildout, not an afterthought once the chemicals show up for the first job.

## Why This List Matters Before You Shop for a Rig

A quoted "rig price" from a seller sometimes includes this full supporting buildout and sometimes doesn't — confirming exactly what's included before comparing prices between sellers avoids the common trap of comparing a fully-equipped rig against a bare proportioner-plus-trailer quote and thinking one seller is dramatically cheaper than they actually are.
